# Frozen-position matrix: does LAND hold up? (issue 81)

Status: results note, 2026-10-30. Written by Claude from the recordings; the maintainer decides
what it means for ADR 0003 and ADR 0004.

## What was run

The ADR 0003 experiment (a frozen GNSS position that stays latched, one vehicle, a 600 m survey
route) repeated with a scripted operator action after the fault: no action, LAND, HOLD, RTL, at
cruise speeds of 5 and 12 m/s and operator delays of 5 and 30 s, three runs per cell, each in a
freshly restarted simulator (`sim/record_frozen_matrix.sh`, PX4 pinned at a427a2e). The four
earlier single runs at 10 m/s and 15 s delay (the ADR 0003 conditions) are included as their own
cells. Recordings with ground truth are in `tests/replay/data/experiments/matrix/`. Every run
was capped at 220 s.

Definitions (thresholds are proposals): *landed* is ground truth altitude at or below 0.5 m
before the recording ended. *Controlled* is landed with a horizontal speed of at most 5 m/s,
measured as the mean over the window from 3 m altitude to touchdown (ADR 0003 quoted 2.9 m/s for
LAND, 31.7 for HOLD and 24.6 for RTL). Counts and ranges only, never rates. The tables below
are generated by `python -m eval.frozen_matrix tests/replay/data/experiments/matrix`, and a test
fails if they differ from the recordings.

## What the numbers show

- **No action:** 12 of 13 runs were still flying at 220 s, 2997 to 8179 m from the start. One
  reached the ground at 39.9 m/s (ground truth only; the recordings carry no flight-mode events,
  so why is not known).
- **Every action got the vehicle to the ground** (13 of 13 each). LAND did so soonest and most
  consistently: 8 to 12 s after the command, against 5 to 62 s for HOLD and 14 to 29 s for RTL.
- **Distance is mostly set by the operator delay, not the action.** LAND and RTL overlap
  (258 to 973 m and 236 to 909 m). At a 5 s delay LAND reached 258 to 443 m and at 30 s 666 to
  973 m. HOLD ranges from 536 to 10007 m, farthest at a 5 s delay (5 and 12 m/s).
- **A controlled touchdown is the exception for every action.** LAND 2 of 13 (both at 5 m/s with
  a 5 s delay, 2.0 and 3.7 m/s), HOLD 1 of 13, RTL 0 of 13. LAND at 30 s delay or 12 m/s
  touched down at 5.7 to 28.3 m/s. At the ADR 0003 conditions (10 m/s, 15 s) the repeat gave
  9.5 m/s for LAND, against 2.9 m/s in the ADR run.

## Against ADR 0003 and ADR 0004

Held: with no action the vehicle keeps flying away; LAND is the quickest way to the ground
(8 to 12 s in all 13 runs).

Not held: ADR 0003's "LAND ... stopped, 2.9 m/s" does not generalise. In 11 of 13 LAND runs the
vehicle touched down at more than 5 m/s, and LAND was no better than RTL on the farthest distance.
ADR 0003 also tried HOLD and RTL only once each, and HOLD's range here is far wider than its
one run (614 m).

ADR 0004 says that if this issue contradicts ADR 0003, its items 2 (LAND is the only action) and
4 are reopened. The contradiction is partial: LAND is still the best of the three on time to the
ground, but not on distance against RTL or on touchdown speed. Whether "quickest to the ground"
is enough to keep LAND as the only action is a decision for the maintainer.

A proposed amendment to the Limits section of ADR 0003, for the maintainer to accept or change:
"Repeated in issue 81 (52 runs over speed and delay): LAND reached the ground 8 to 12 s after the
command in every run, but touched down at 2.0 to 28.3 m/s; the 2.9 m/s above was the best case.
See docs/validation/frozen-position-matrix.md."

## Limits

Three runs per cell (one for the 10 m/s cell), one simulator, one vehicle, PX4's `stuck` fault
model, a 220 s cap (distances are the farthest reached by the end of the recording), and a 5 m/s
controlled threshold that is a proposal. The touchdown speed is a mean over
a window, not an instantaneous speed. Why the vehicle behaves this way after LAND is not
examined: the recordings have ground truth but no flight-mode events.
