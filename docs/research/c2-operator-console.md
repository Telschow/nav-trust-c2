# Research: C2 operator console design for human-on-the-loop supervision

First pass 2026-10-24. All sources accessed 2026-10-24. Public, unclassified, non-weapon material only. Quotes are from the owner's page or file. Anything that could not be read from the owner is listed under gaps and not used as evidence. Proposals are labelled as proposals.

## Question

What do public human-factors standards, regulation, research and first-party ground-station documentation say about the design of consoles that supervise uncrewed vehicles with a human on the loop, and which concrete layout and UI choices could the nav-trust-c2 console adopt to be more realistic and more defensible?

## Short answer

- The most usable public requirements come from crewed-aircraft alerting regulation (14 CFR 25.1322) and the NASA human factors guidelines for remote pilot stations, which copy that rule and add link-loss items. Together they give a three-level priority scheme, red and amber meanings, a second sensory channel, acknowledge and suppress, removal when the condition clears, and an annunciation of suppression.
- NASA's guidelines say the station should alert when link is lost and when it resumes, use a unique sound for lost control link, and show link footprint. They also require two distinct, dissimilar actions to start flight termination, and say alerts must not be subject to silent failure.
- The console already has more than QGroundControl or Mission Planner document: a persistent decision band, shape plus text for trust state, last-known marking for NO LINK, an expiry countdown, and an explicit "if you approve" list.
- Gaps against the sources: no second sensory channel, no acknowledge or shelve state, an expired decision simply reappears with no visible history of having been missed, no link-lost or link-resumed event, and no visible uncertainty growth for a vehicle that is not heard.
- Both round 1 participants let a decision expire twice. The sources support making an unanswered, re-raised alert look different from a new one and adding a second channel. They give no number for how long an expiry should be.
- The classic papers (Endsley 1995, Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens 2000, Lee and See 2004), ISA-18.2 text, EEMUA 191 and MIL-STD-1472H could not be read from their owners (HTTP 403 or paywalled). The note does not paraphrase them.
- Open-source GCS documentation is thin on alarms and link loss. QGroundControl documents a flight-status indicator with colours, a "Communication Lost" state, press-and-hold confirmation and an indoor dark scheme. Mission Planner documents a link-quality percentage and a messages tab. Neither documents acknowledge, repeat or shelving.

## Findings

### 1. Layout and information hierarchy for several vehicles

- QGroundControl Fly View is built from a view selector, status indicators, action tools, video, an instrument panel, an attitude display and a map. The map "shows all connected vehicles' positions and the current vehicle's mission". The page does not say what is always visible. [QGC Fly View](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/fly_view/fly_view.html), 2026-10-24.
- The QGC toolbar carries a Multi-Vehicle Selector next to Flight Status, Flight Mode, GPS, Battery and Telemetry and RC RSSI. So one vehicle is "current" and the rest are reached through a selector. [QGC Fly View toolbar](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/fly_view/fly_view_toolbar.html), 2026-10-24. The dedicated multi-vehicle page returned HTTP 404.
- Mission Planner's Flight Data screen is a single-vehicle HUD plus map. Double-clicking the HUD pops it out to a second screen. [Mission Planner Flight Data](https://ardupilot.org/planner/docs/mission-planner-ground-control-station.html), 2026-10-24.
- NASA guidelines G_13: functions needed under usual flight "should be located in the pilot's primary field-of-view". G_14: "Warnings and cautions should not be obscured by other RPS displays". G_15 requires an equivalent level of safety to show a parameter only part of the time instead of full time. [NASA RPS guidelines v1.1 (Hobbs and Lyall)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190000211), 2026-10-24. The text was read from the PDF; guideline numbers are the document's own.
- NASA lists "visual clutter, display competition for attention and the prioritization of information" as emergent display properties to design for (same document, general guidelines section).
- NATO RTO-TR-HFM-170 (supervisory control of multiple uninhabited systems) is the obvious military source. Its PDF could not be fetched (HTTP 404 on the sto.nato.int path; DTIC copy HTTP 403). Only a search summary was seen. Not used.

Implication: the QGC and Mission Planner models are single-vehicle first. A fleet-row list with one map is the console's own choice and is not contradicted by anything read. The NASA rule that warnings stay unobscured supports keeping the decision band outside the scrolling panes.

### 2. Alerting and alarm management

- 14 CFR 25.1322 (crewed transport aircraft, quoted verbatim in the NASA guidelines as G_9): three levels, "Warning: ... immediate flightcrew awareness and immediate flightcrew response", "Caution: ... immediate awareness and subsequent response", "Advisory: ... awareness and may require subsequent response". Warning and caution alerts must be prioritised within category, give "attention-getting cues through at least two different senses", and "permit each occurrence ... to be acknowledged and suppressed, unless they are required to be continuous". Alerts must "be removed when the alerting condition no longer exists". [eCFR 14 CFR 25.1322](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-25.1322), 2026-10-24.
- Colour convention: red for warning, amber or yellow for caution, any colour except red or green for advisory (25.1322(e), same page).
- Nuisance: the alert function must "prevent the presentation of an alert that is inappropriate or unnecessary". Any means to suppress must not be "readily available ... so that it could be operated inadvertently or by habitual reflexive action", and "When an alert is suppressed, there must be a clear and unmistakable annunciation" of that (25.1322(d), same page).
- NASA G_10: "Systems that alert the pilot to a critical anomaly should not be subject to a silent failure." [NASA RPS guidelines](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190000211), 2026-10-24.
- HSE (the UK regulator) on process-plant alarms: "Alarms should be prioritised in terms of which alarms require the most urgent operator attention", "The alarm processing should ensure that fleeting or repeating alarms do not result in operator overload", "The presentation of alarms should not exceed that which the operator is capable of acting upon". It names filtering, deadband, debounce timers and shelving as EEMUA 191 techniques and warns that shelving or suppression needs "controls ... to ensure that alarms are returned to an active state when they are relevant". [HSE control systems](https://www.hse.gov.uk/comah/sragtech/techmeascontsyst.htm), 2026-10-24. HSE's alarm page adds "Every alarm should be useful and relevant to the operator, and have a defined response". [HSE alarm management](https://www.hse.gov.uk/humanfactors/topics/alarm-management.htm), 2026-10-24.
- ISA says ANSI/ISA-18.2-2016 covers the "entire alarm lifecycle" and "alarm prioritization and performance monitoring". Its page does not list states, shelving numbers or metrics. [ISA-18 series](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-18-series-of-standards), 2026-10-24.
- Alarm-rate and priority-split numbers attributed to EEMUA 191 (for example "10 alarms per 10 minutes", "5% / 15% / 80%") appeared only in patents, an arXiv review and an enforcement notice. EEMUA's page returned HTTP 403. Not used.
- QGC: "Critical messages appear in red, and warnings and notices in orange"; critical messages also open a "Vehicle Alert" popup; unread info messages turn the status label blue. Settings offer "Mute all audio output". [QGC toolbar](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/fly_view/fly_view_toolbar.html); [QGC general settings](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/settings_view/general.html), 2026-10-24. Neither documents acknowledge, repeat or shelve.
- Mission Planner: the Messages tab "can range in importance from informational to critical" and important messages also appear on the HUD. Which events are spoken is not documented. [Mission Planner Flight Data](https://ardupilot.org/planner/docs/mission-planner-flight-data.html), 2026-10-24.

Lifecycle that the sources jointly support (reading, not a quoted model): raised, annunciated on two senses, acknowledged (cue silenced, indication stays), cleared (removed when condition ends), and any suppression visibly annunciated and self-returning.

### 3. Decision support and levels of automation

- The classic scale of levels of automation (Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens 2000, IEEE; Sheridan) could not be read. The IEEE page returned no content and the MITRE reproduction returned HTTP 403. The console's approve-before-act design is stated in ADR 0004; this note does not claim where it falls on that scale.
- NASA P_2.5.1b: "Two distinct and dissimilar actions of the RPAS crew should be required to initiate the flight termination command", and NASA notes it deliberately removed the STANAG 4671 text that termination controls be "readily available and accessible" because they should not be. P_2.5.1a asks that such controls "minimize the possibility of confusion and subsequent inadvertent operation". [NASA RPS guidelines](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190000211), 2026-10-24. Flight termination is not LAND, but LAND is the console's irreversible command.
- QGC confirms actions by press and hold: "click and hold the button until the action is accepted". No timeout is stated. [QGC Fly Tools](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/fly_view/fly_tools.html), 2026-10-24.
- NASA T_2.5.1 and the surrounding text treat the termination decision as needing "the information pilots will require to make this difficult decision". This supports showing the reason and the consequence next to the button. No source read gives a rationale length.
- Time pressure and expiry: NASA's lost-link description has a decision interval after which an onboard timer acts, because "It would be disruptive if the RPA started to fly its lost link procedure each time a brief link interruption occurred". [NASA RPS guidelines](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190000211), 2026-10-24. This is about the vehicle, not about operator prompts. No source read says how long an operator prompt should live or what to do when it lapses.
- Repo evidence: in round 1 both participants let the card expire twice; one approved only after a hint. [findings-2026-10](../usability/findings-2026-10.md).

### 4. Situation awareness

- Endsley 1995 (Human Factors 37(1)) is the origin of the three levels (perceive, comprehend, project). Its page returned HTTP 403, so none of its content is quoted here. NASA cites Endsley and Jones, "Designing for situation awareness" (2012), for general HMI principles such as managing data overload and avoiding competing alarms, without reproducing it.
- What can be sourced: NASA G_11 asks for a work environment that "maintains pilot engagement" during extended low workload, which fits a mostly-GREEN fleet. NASA T_1.5.6 to T_1.5.8 ask the station to keep the pilot aware of link strength, abnormalities and latency, and to let them anticipate degradation.
- Glanceability: QGC puts GPS, battery, telemetry RSSI and flight status in one always-present toolbar strip, and uses the status label colour (yellow, red, orange, blue) to summarise the worst state ([toolbar page](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/fly_view/fly_view_toolbar.html)). That is the single-vehicle form of "worst state wins".

### 5. Trust and uncertainty display

- Lee and See 2004 (trust in automation, calibration) could not be read (HTTP 403). Not paraphrased.
- Mission Planner shows GPS "hdop, sats" under the map as reception quality, and QGC has a GPS indicator. Neither documentation says an error bound is drawn on the map. [Mission Planner Flight Data](https://ardupilot.org/planner/docs/mission-planner-flight-data.html), 2026-10-24.
- NASA I_1.5.12: link latency "in milliseconds", where relevant. I_1.5.13: information "to anticipate link degradations", which "may include link footprint, including areas that may be affected by terrain masking". P_1.5.3 and P_1.5.4 put the datalink footprint on the map. [NASA RPS guidelines](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190000211), 2026-10-24.
- No owner source read prescribes how to draw a position error bound, how to label calibrated against raw confidence, or how fast an uncertainty region should grow. The console's calibrated bound with measured coverage (GLOSSARY, "Position bound") is the project's own method. Any ring that grows with time since last contact would be a project design choice using the vehicle's maximum speed, which must come from the simulation, not from this note.

### 6. Lost-link and degraded-link display

- NASA I_1.5.7: "The RPS should alert the pilot when the link is lost." I_1.5.15: "inform the pilot when a lost link is resumed." I_1.5.6: alert when the aircraft is "approaching an area where link is likely to be lost". I_1.5.10: alert on interference. C_1.5.4: let the pilot set "the duration of a link outage that must occur before a lost link response is triggered". [NASA RPS guidelines](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190000211), 2026-10-24.
- NASA P_1.5.1 quotes STANAG 4671 (via NASA): "There must be an alert for the UAS crew, via a clear and distinct aural and visual signal, for any total loss of the command and control data link". P_1.5.2: the aural warning for lost control link "should be a unique sound, not also used to signify other conditions". P_1.5.7 and P_1.5.8: payload link quality and its sound must be separate and distinguishable from the control link.
- NASA's lost-link description has three stages, the first being that the aircraft "continues to fly in accordance with the last command received", an onboard timer, then the procedure. See also [lost-link-authority.md](lost-link-authority.md) for the NASA Ames T0 to T3 framing and for PX4 and ArduPilot behaviour. [Sakakeeny et al., NTRS 20240007906](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240007906), 2026-10-24.
- QGC Flight Status shows "Communication Lost": "QGroundControl has lost communication with the vehicle". Telemetry RSSI shows local and remote RSSI and radio link quality. The page gives no last-known position, time since contact or predicted position. [QGC toolbar](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/fly_view/fly_view_toolbar.html), 2026-10-24.
- Mission Planner shows link quality as an "averaged percentage of good packets", no thresholds given. [Mission Planner Flight Data](https://ardupilot.org/planner/docs/mission-planner-ground-control-station.html), 2026-10-24.
- Failsafe state announced: neither GCS documentation page read describes how a failsafe in progress is announced. Not claimed.
- Stale data in general: no owner source found beyond NASA G_10 (no silent failure). The console's "last known" with age is the project's own convention.

### 7. Audit, event log and shift handover

- QGC saves telemetry logs: "Telemetry logs (`.tlog`) automatically saved ... after flight" (setting "Save log after each flight"). [QGC general settings](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/settings_view/general.html), 2026-10-24. The log is a vehicle data record, not an operator decision record.
- NASA notes that in-flight transfer of control "can be a time of particular risk, associated with system mode errors and coordination breakdowns", with a case of "inadvertent transfer of control between RPS ... due to controls set in error". [NASA RPS guidelines](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20190000211), 2026-10-24.
- HSE on shift handover: it should be "conducted face-to-face", "two-way, with both participants taking joint responsibility", use "both verbal and written communication", and the support equipment such as "logs and computer displays" should be designed "with consideration of the operators needs". It has three elements: preparation by outgoing staff, a live exchange, and checking by the incoming person. [HSE shift handover](https://www.hse.gov.uk/humanfactors/topics/shift-handover.htm), 2026-10-24.
- UK CAA CAP3154 proposes a periodic log of "which connections are active and the status of all connections". See [lost-link-authority.md](lost-link-authority.md).

### 8. Accessibility, colour and audio

- WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1: "Color is not used as the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response" or "distinguishing a visual element". [W3C Use of Color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html), 2026-10-24.
- WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11 asks 3:1 contrast against adjacent colours for "Visual information required to identify user interface components and states" and parts of graphics needed to understand content. [W3C Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html), 2026-10-24. WCAG is a web standard, not an operations-room standard; it is cited for the contrast and redundancy rules only.
- Colour-blind guidance from the University of Tokyo Color Universal Design page (Okabe and Ito): "Do not convey information in color only", use "a combination of different shapes, positions, line types and coloring patterns", avoid red and green together, and note that dark red on dark backgrounds can look almost black to protanopes. [Okabe and Ito, Color Universal Design](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/), 2026-10-24. Hosted at the University of Cologne; palette itself is an image and was not read.
- 25.1322(e) uses red and amber for warning and caution and allows "visual coding techniques" where colour is unavailable.
- QGC offers "Indoor (Dark)" and "Outdoor (Light)" colour schemes and "Mute all audio output". [QGC general settings](https://docs.qgroundcontrol.com/master/en/qgc-user-guide/settings_view/general.html), 2026-10-24. No source read gives a luminance or contrast value for operations rooms. MIL-STD-1472H could not be found from its owner; only search results about older revisions appeared.

## What nav-trust-c2 already does

From the components read (VehicleRow, StateChip, TrustStrip, LinkBadge, DecisionBanner, RecommendationCard, ConfirmDialog, Header, ActivityLog, MapView):

- Fleet as one row per vehicle: trust state as colour, shape (circle, triangle, square) and words, reason in plain text, calibrated position error with coverage, time in state, sectors, margin to alert, two-minute trust strip. Matches WCAG 1.4.1 and the redundancy advice.
- A solid red "Decision required" band across the screen while any decision waits, with the time left and a Review button. `role="alert"` on the text only, not the countdown.
- A decision card with the reason, an urgency note, an "If you approve" list, a countdown bar, and Deny and Approve. Approve opens a confirm dialog with the consequence, the operator's name and a demo notice. Expired cards are disabled and say "Expired, no longer actionable".
- NO LINK is its own badge in the accent colour, not a trust colour. The row shows "Last known", "Last heard N s ago", dashes out the chip and replaces bound and margin with "not current". The header counts "no link". The map key explains the no-link colour and a dashed ring "do not rely on this position".
- Header: fleet counts by state, LIVE or replay label, DISCONNECTED indicator, backend clock.
- Activity log with `aria-live="polite"`, a time, a tone mark and text.
- No sound, no acknowledge state, no shelving, no handover view. (Searched the components read; not a full-repo audit.)

## Proposed changes to the console

All items are proposals. Order is by expected value for the operator against the sources. Each is meant to fit one GitHub issue. None changes the ICD or adds a dependency. Trust-state colours and density stay first: each item adds text, shape or a small element, not a new colour for trust.

1. **Mark a re-raised decision as re-raised.** Component: `DecisionBanner`, `RecommendationCard`, store. Source: 25.1322(d) on nuisance and HSE "fleeting or repeating alarms"; repo finding that both participants let two cards expire. Change: the band and card say "Raised again, previous one expired unanswered at T+...", with a count, instead of looking new; the activity log groups the repeats. Does not change the 60 s expiry. Effort: M.
2. **Add an acknowledge state to the decision band, separate from approve and deny.** Component: `DecisionBanner`, `RecommendationCard`. Source: 25.1322(c)(3) and (a)(3). Change: "Seen" silences any cue and changes the band wording to "Seen, waiting for decision", but the band stays until the recommendation is decided, expires or the condition clears. Acknowledge must be logged and must not be possible to mistake for approval. Effort: M. Needs a maintainer decision on whether acknowledgement belongs in the audit log or ADR 0004 wording.
3. **Add a second sensory channel with a visible mute.** Component: new small audio module, `Header`. Source: 25.1322(c)(2) (two senses), (d) (suppression annunciated); NASA P_1.5.2 (unique sound for lost control link); QGC mute option. Change: one tone for "decision required", a different one for "link lost", off by default in demo, with a visible "Muted" tag in the header. Needs the browser autoplay rule handled. Effort: M.
4. **Announce link lost and link resumed as events.** Component: `ActivityLog`, `Header`, store. Source: NASA I_1.5.7 and I_1.5.15, P_1.5.1. Change: an alert-toned log line and a short banner note when a vehicle goes NO LINK and when it returns, naming what it did meanwhile once ADR 0004 reports exist. Effort: S.
5. **Keep an expired decision visible until the operator sees it.** Component: `RecommendationCard`, `DecidedRow`. Source: NASA G_10 (no silent failure). Change: an expired card moves to the decided list as "Expired, nobody answered" with the vehicle's state now, and stays pinned for a short period or until acknowledged, rather than only being replaced. Effort: S.
6. **Fix the fleet summary on narrow screens.** Component: `Header`. Source: NASA G_13, G_15 (full-time display unless equivalent safety is shown). Change: the summary and the no-link count are hidden below the `md` breakpoint; keep a compact count always visible. Effort: S.
7. **Show console-to-backend loss on the rows.** Component: `VehicleRow`, `App`. Source: NASA G_10. Change: check that a DISCONNECTED header also marks every row "last known"; at present the header shows DISCONNECTED and a separate notice, and the row code reads only the vehicle link state. If rows are not marked, mark them. Effort: S. Verify first.
8. **Draw the last-known position distinctly and show its age on the map.** Component: `MapView`. Source: NASA P_1.5.3 and P_1.5.4 (footprint on the map) and the console's own "last known" convention. Change: a no-link vehicle gets a hollow marker with an age label ("last heard 42 s ago"). A growing ring (maximum speed times age) is optional and needs a documented speed from the simulation; label it as a bound on how far it could have moved, not a prediction. Effort: M.
9. **Raise confirmation friction for LAND only.** Component: `ConfirmDialog`. Source: NASA P_2.5.1a and P_2.5.1b (two dissimilar actions for termination); QGC press-and-hold. Change: today the dialog focuses "Confirm approval" on open, so a single Enter completes approval after one click. Options: press and hold the confirm button, or move default focus to Cancel. Must be tested against the time-to-decision metric in `docs/usability/`, since round 1 showed the opposite problem (decisions not made). Effort: S.
10. **Check non-text contrast of state chips.** Component: `StateChip`, `TrustStrip`, `VehicleRow`. Source: WCAG 1.4.11 (3:1), Okabe and Ito (dark red on dark). Change: chip borders use 40 to 65 percent opacity and the strip uses 0.85 opacity; measure against the panel background and raise where under 3:1. Shape and text already carry the state, so this is a refinement. Effort: S.
11. **Per-priority wording that mirrors warning, caution, advisory.** Component: `language`, `DecisionBanner`, `RecommendationCard`. Source: 25.1322(b) and (e). Change: RED recommendation = warning (red, "Decision required"), AMBER with no action = advisory (no band, no cue). Write the mapping into `console/DESIGN.md` (maintainer). Effort: S.
12. **Shift handover summary.** Component: new panel reading the audit log. Source: HSE shift handover (written plus verbal, logs designed for the operator). Change: one printable page: open decisions, non-GREEN and NO LINK vehicles with ages, approved contingencies, last N events. Effort: L.
13. **Add a dark-theme statement to DESIGN.md.** Component: `console/DESIGN.md` (maintainer). Source: QGC indoor dark scheme; DESIGN.md says its source has no dark theme. Change: record that the dark panel palette is the operations default and how its state colours were chosen. Effort: S.
14. **Re-test items 1 to 3 in round 2.** Component: `docs/usability/`. Source: repo findings. Change: add "was the missed decision noticed after a re-raise" and "sound on vs off" to the session sheet. Effort: S.

## Gaps and what could not be verified

- Endsley 1995 (Sage, HTTP 403), Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens 2000 (IEEE page empty; MITRE copy HTTP 403), Lee and See 2004 (Sage, HTTP 403): not read, not paraphrased. Only bibliographic details were seen in search results. Their content, including the levels of automation scale and calibration of trust, is therefore absent from the findings.
- EEMUA 191 (eemua.org HTTP 403): alarm rate and priority distribution targets not verified. ISA-18.2 text is paywalled; only the owner's summary page was read. IEC 62682 not fetched.
- NUREG-0700 (nrc.gov HTTP 403), FAA HF-STD-001B and the FAA Human Factors Workbench alarm page (redirected to a 404), MIL-STD-1472H (not located from the owner; only older revisions in search results): not used.
- NATO RTO-TR-HFM-170 and STO-MP-HFM-217 on supervising several uninhabited vehicles: the STO URL returned HTTP 404 and the DTIC copy HTTP 403. Only search summaries were seen. STANAG 4671 is quoted only through the NASA guidelines.
- NASA guidelines were read from the PDF with a text extractor. Guideline numbers are as printed; page numbers are not given because the extraction offsets them.
- QGC multi-vehicle page returned HTTP 404. Neither QGC nor Mission Planner documentation pages read describe acknowledge, repeat, shelve, failsafe announcement, last-known position or predicted search area. That absence is of the pages read, not of the software.
- No owner source read covers: position error bound drawing on maps, calibrated versus raw confidence display, uncertainty growth after data loss, an operator prompt expiry time, or how much rationale to show. Items 8 and 9 above rest on analogy and on repo findings, not on a standard.
- 14 CFR 25.1322 applies to crewed transport aircraft. NASA reuses it for remote pilot stations; this project is a small SAR fleet and is not bound by it. It is cited as the best public statement of alert priority and lifecycle.
- Round 1 usability data are two people, a convenience sample, facilitated by the developer. They motivate items 1, 2 and 5 but do not prove them.
